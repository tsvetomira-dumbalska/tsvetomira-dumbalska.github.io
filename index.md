## About

I am a postdoctoral research fellow at the University of Oxford. My research aims to understand the psychological impacts of the modern digital world. How do digital media and AI shape our learning, motivation and behavior, both during digital engagement and afterwards? My work draws links between psychology and economics, and psychology and computer science, combining randomized controlled experiments with computational modeling and machine learning. You can learn more about my research and publications <a href="/research">here</a> and my teaching and service <a href="/teaching_service">here</a>.
<br>
<br>
Before this, I completed a BA in Economics at Brown University and a DPhil (PhD) in Experimental Psychology at the University of Oxford. My <a href="/thesis">doctoral work</a> investigated why irrelevant contextual information can often sway our choices. It showed that many of these effects arise from adaptation, the brain's tendency to recalibrate to its most recent context, a principle that may also explain the lingering aftereffects of digital environments on our motivation and on how we feel. Outside the lab, you can find me rollerblading, ice skating or jogging in nature and savoring a good book over some iced tea.
