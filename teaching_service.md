## Teaching and Service

### Teaching
Teaching is one of the most rewarding parts of my work. As Stipendiary Lecturer in Psychology at Brasenose College, I provide tutorial teaching and pastoral care to around 16 undergraduates and lead undergraduate admissions. I hold teaching accreditation for Descriptor 1 of the UK Professional Standards Framework for Teaching and Supporting Learning in Higher Education (Associate Fellowship of the Higher Education Academy). 

Over the past 10 years, I have taught across the full Oxford psychology curriculum, from Prelims to Part B, MSci and graduate level. This includes tutorials in Introduction to Psychology, Probability Theory and Statistics, Individual Differences and Clinical Psychology, Social Psychology, Behavioural Neuroscience, Cognition, and AI, Cognition and Society. I designed and convene a course on Computational Psychology and Artificial Intelligence for Lady Margaret Hall’s summer program, and developed lecture material on the replication crisis and open science for the Part A Social Psychology course. My students have gone on to receive awards for top results in their undergraduate examinations.

Since 2019, I have supervised 19 students, from undergraduate to doctoral level, including DPhil and visiting PhD students, MSci and final-year research project students, library dissertation students, research assistants and summer interns. My undergraduate students’ research has been supported by the Biomedical Vacation Scheme, the UNIQ+ Graduate Access Scheme and college funding programs at Hertford and Wadham, and five of them have published their research.

### Service
I am deeply committed to creating an inclusive, supportive community for everyone. I believe in direct anti-racist action and have co-organized a University-wide discussion group on decolonizing research. I have supported college- and University-led widening access initiatives, including the OxNet summer school and the UNIQ+ research internship program.

At the departmental level, I have chaired the Early Career Researcher Committee and served on the DEI and People & Culture committees. In college, I have served as a welfare officer and held a range of committee roles, and I am a trained peer supporter.

My service has been recognized by the W. H. Pembroke Prize for Service to the Community and the Department of Experimental Psychology’s Award for Contributions to Research Culture and Career Development.
